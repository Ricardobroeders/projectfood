# Pillar 3 — "Healthy eating habits": clusters, slugs and keyword map

**In one line:** One pillar on the adult's head question ("how do I build healthy eating habits
that stick") and six clusters, each a distinct question on the way from that problem to the app:
how long it takes, why it is hard, habit stacking, the grocery shelf, never missing twice, and
eating more vegetables one taste at a time. The spine is Clear's four laws, said in our words;
every page ends in the one-minute log and the app, without a promise. Backlog item 27's search
half ([[strategy-backlog]]); the product pillar "How it works" is a separate page on its own
route and is not in this plan.

> queries). **Volumes and difficulty are not in**: the DataForSEO connector is unauthorised (item

## The reader

The adult who holds the phone ([[decision-2026-10-07-two-ways-to-use-it]]), counting for
themselves or for everyone at the table. Never "the parent" by default. The pages say "you";
where a table with children changes the advice, one sentence says so and links pillar 1.

## Naming and slugs

healthy eating habits"; the slug carries the topic, the title carries the phrase). Cluster slugs
carry the query, never "kids" or "children" (hub-slug rule, [[seo-site-architecture]]). Internal

Sections that stay inside the pillar (not pages): identity and the vote, motivation versus
environment, tracking without calories, the household version (one account, the kid as the
reward), when to ask for help. Excluded on purpose: weight loss, diets by name, calorie counting,
supplements, "cheat days", anything clinical.

## Why these clusters

A cluster is a page when the query is phrased differently from the pillar, page 1 is a different
set of sites, and the answer needs its own artefact (a number, a rule, a list). "How long" has a
number; "why hard" has a mechanism; "habit stacking" has a formula; "grocery" has a list; "never
miss twice" has a rule; "more vegetables" has the taste count. What fails the test (identity,
tracking, the kid at the table) is an H2 in the pillar.

## Sources for this pillar

Habit science is not on [[brand-stats-and-claims]] yet; the rows below are proposed there on
2026-10-10 and used in the English drafts pending Ricardo's confirmation:

- Lally P, van Jaarsveld CHM, Potts HWW, Wardle J (2010). How are habits formed: modelling habit
  formation in the real world. *European Journal of Social Psychology* 40(6):998–1009.
  doi:10.1002/ejsp.674. 96 adults chose one daily eating, drinking or activity habit; automaticity
  took a median of 66 days to plateau, with a range of 18 to 254 days; missing a single day made no
  material difference.
- Gardner B, Lally P, Wardle J (2012). Making health habitual: the psychology of 'habit-formation'
  and general practice. *British Journal of General Practice* 62(605):664–666.
  doi:10.3399/bjgp12X659466. Advice for patients: pick a small action, tie it to one consistent
  daily context cue, repeat; expect about 10 weeks before it feels automatic.
- Clear J (2018). *Atomic Habits*. Avery. The four laws (obvious, attractive, easy, satisfying),
  habit stacking, never miss twice, the Goldilocks rule. Named once per page, ideas in our words,
  never quoted, no endorsement implied.
- Already on the stats page: McDonald et al. 2018 (30 plants, adults, observational) and the
  children's tasting numbers (Wardle 2003, Cooke 2011, Dovey 2008) for the one table-with-kids
  sentence per page.
