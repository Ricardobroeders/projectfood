# Stats against the typical household (decided 2026-10-07)

**Decision.** The stats screen shows the household against the typical household on Project
Food, in the charts and never as a verdict: **Your mix** (a horizontal bar per category, your
share of everything logged, the typical share as a mark on the track), the **plants per week**
bars with two lines (the goal at 30, the typical week dashed), and **plants per day** as a line
with the typical day dashed through it. The four record tiles carry their own tint (the streak
in the Home chip's yellow, the longest run in gold, weeks at 30 in green, best week in blue) and
get a render each, Ricardo's. Shipped over the air as 1.0.31. Ricardo's cut of brainstorm 25,
with two changes to Claude's proposal: the comparison lives in the chart, not in a sentence, and
the day chart becomes a line.

**The benchmark.** `projectfood-mobile/src/data/benchmarks.json`, generated by
`supabase/scripts/benchmarks.sql` and refreshed with an update, never a live query across
households. Medians over the family-app era (logs since 2026-09-07), so a bulk day moves
nothing: typical week 22 distinct plants, typical logging day 8, typical first week 17, category
share vegetable 41, fruit 19, nuts and seeds 16, herbs 9, whole grains 5, ferments 5, legumes 4
(2026-10-07). The app says "typical" ("Gemiddeld", "Media") and explains it once: the middle of
all households on Project Food. Household counts are never shown ([[brand-stats-and-claims]]).

**Season.** One Europe table for every locale (Ricardo: "focus on Europe, don't make it more
complex"): 106 plants carry months (`supabase/scripts/season-europe.py`), staples, mushrooms,
imports, grains, ferments and most nuts show "All year". The Log page has an "In season" tab
after "All" and a leaf on the row's tile; nothing is measured or compared yet. A season stat
waits until the table has earned trust.

**Why.** Testers, the solo adults above all, asked for their own behaviour against an average
(row 3). The category mix is the one chart that changes what people buy: legumes and whole grains
are the gap for nearly everyone. Joy-not-guilt: a thin mark next to your own number, no red or
green, no "below average".

**Held back** (parking lot): new plants this month vs typical, plant-family coverage, the colour
mix, days logged per week. Never: ranking members, streak percentiles.
