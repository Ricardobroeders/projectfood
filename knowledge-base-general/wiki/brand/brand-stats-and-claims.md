# Stats and claims

**In one line:** the numbers Project Food may use in public copy, each with its source, its date
and the sentence we use it in; the numbers and claims we never publish; how to cite.

> First draft 2026-09-24. Sources verified on that date (DOIs resolve, quotes checked on the
> pages). A number that is not on this page does not go into copy: propose the source to

## 2. Numbers we never publish

- User counts, households, retention, streak averages, "families already tasting".
- Semrush volumes and difficulty, Search Console clicks, Play Console installs.
- Testimonials or quotes that are not verbatim and approved by the person.
- Our own aggregated tasting data ("tasted by 1,200 kids") until the privacy gate is written:
  aggregates only, minimum group sizes, never per household ([[seo-content-types]] format 7).

---

## 3. Claims we never make

- Health outcomes for children: gut, immune, growth, weight, mood, sleep, concentration, skin.
- "Improves gut health", "boosts", "healthier kids", "a healthier family".
- Diagnosis or treatment: ARFID, sensory feeding disorder, allergies, failure to thrive, eating
  disorders. When a case reads clinical, point to the doctor, the consultatiebureau (JGZ) or the
  pediatra, in one sentence, without humour.
- "Cure", "fix", "solve" picky eating. We help a child learn to eat more different things.
- "Free."
- "Proven", "scientifically proven", "guaranteed", "100%". Say "in the studies", cited.
- "Superfood" as a benefit. The app's Superfood pill is a category label and never comes with a
  benefit sentence.
- "Good gut, good life" is a brand line and stays. "Boosts gut diversity" is a claim and goes
  (the live home page still carries it; item 14).

## 4. How to cite

- **Inline:** author and year in brackets at the end of the sentence, once per claim: "(Cooke,
  2011)". No footnote numbers in body text.
- **In the article file:** the `citations` front matter (author, title, year, doi, url). The page
  renders them under "Sources" with the DOI link.
- **One science paragraph per article.** The rest is practice.
- **Institutions by name** when they are the reader's own reference: Voedingscentrum (NL), AAP
  and CDC (EN), the pediatra and the Ministero della Salute (IT). Only for things they actually
  say, with the page linked and the date checked.
- **Date any number that can change:** "(checked 2026-09-24)". Product numbers included.
- **If it cannot be sourced from this page, do not write it.** Silence beats an unverifiable
  sentence.

## 5. Contradictions to handle honestly

- **Stickers.** The Voedingscentrum's plan says "beloon liever niet met toetjes of stickers";
  Cooke 2011 found a sticker for tasting helped and did no harm at three months. Our position:
  reward the taste, never the clean plate, never with food. A tick on the chart or a card is a
  record of a taste, not a bribe. In NL, say both, because the reader knows the Voedingscentrum
  page.
- **Reward versus exposure.** An earlier trial (Wardle, Herrera, Cooke & Gibson 2003, *EJCN*,
  ages 5 to 7, doi 10.1038/sj.ejcn.1601541) found exposure alone did better than exposure plus a
  reward; the later and larger Cooke 2011 trial found the reward did not undermine liking. Say:
  the taste is the mechanic, the reward marks the taste.
- **How many times.** CDC says 8 to 10, the AAP says "10 or more", the trials used 12 to 14
  daily tastes. We say "8 to 15, sometimes more", and that a lick counts.
- **The 30 number is an adult observation.** Observational, self-reported diet, adults. On the
  family site it is the guideline we count towards; the app never says it does something for a
  child.
