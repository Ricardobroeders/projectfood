# Humour

**In one line:** dry, one line, in the intro. Serious does not mean joyless; the smile says
"we have stood at that table too".

> Decided by Ricardo on 2026-09-24 (options were: none, dry one-liner in intros, playful
> throughout). Loaded with [[brand-voice]] by the `pf-voice` skill.

---

## The rule

One dry sentence per piece, at most, and only in the places listed below. If a parent would not
recognise the line as true, cut it. If it needs an exclamation mark to land, it does not land.

---

## Where it belongs

- **Article introductions.** One sentence, inside or right after the answer-first opening.
- **A tips section.** One dry observation at most, on the situation, not the reader.
- **Achievement names and celebration copy in the app.** This is where the playful register
  lives: "Full table", "Big dinner", "Nice try" (the one that fires at 50 plants in a day).
  Two words, sentence case, a wink.
- **Social captions.** One line.

## Where it never goes

- FAQ answers. The reader wants the answer.
- Meta titles and meta descriptions. The reader is scanning.
- Notifications. A question, nothing else.
- Red-flag and referral passages (when to call the consultatiebureau, the pediatra, the doctor).
- Anything about a child's difficulty, weight, health or a diagnosis.
- Privacy, terms, support, account deletion, system email.
- Error states and empty states.
- The store listing. The register there is plain.

---

## The style

- **Dry, not slapstick.** An understated observation, not a joke with a punchline.
- **A recognisable dinner truth.** The pea that touched the potato. The "I don't like it" that
  arrives before the fork moves. The yoghurt that is fine on Tuesday and impossible on Wednesday.
- **Never at the child's expense.** The child is doing something normal.
- **Never at the parent's expense.** The parent is tired and doing their best.
- **The target of the smile:** the situation, the clean-plate rule, the dessert negotiation, the
  parenting-advice industry, ourselves.
- **No vegetable puns.** No "lettuce", no "turnip the beet", no "peas and love".
- **No emoji as the joke. No sarcasm. No irony that needs explaining.**
- **One sentence.** Then move on.

---

## The built-in irony to lean on

The child who pushes a carrot away at dinner will go looking for one when a card is at stake.
Nothing about the carrot changed. That gap between the plate and the game is the product, and
it is fair to say so once in a while.

The second one: about ten small tastes is most of the trick, and nobody tells parents.

## Examples

### On brand

| Locale | Line |
|---|---|
| EN | A two-year-old can tell from across the room that the broccoli has touched the potato. |
| EN | Your child does not hate carrots. Your child has met a carrot four times and needs about ten more introductions. |
| EN | The same child who negotiates every pea will go looking for one when there is a card to earn. |
| NL | Een peuter ziet vanaf de andere kant van de tafel dat de broccoli de aardappel heeft aangeraakt. |
| NL | Je kind haat geen wortel. Je kind heeft een wortel vier keer ontmoet en heeft nog een stuk of tien kennismakingen nodig. |
| NL | Hetzelfde kind dat over elke erwt onderhandelt, gaat er zelf een zoeken als er een kaart mee te verdienen is. |
| IT | Un bambino di 2 anni vede dall'altra parte della tavola che il broccolo ha toccato la patata. |
| IT | Tuo figlio non odia le carote. Ha incontrato una carota quattro volte e gliene servono ancora una decina. |
| IT | Lo stesso bambino che tratta su ogni pisello ne va a cercare uno da solo quando c'è una carta da guadagnare. |

### Off brand

| Line | Why |
|---|---|
| Lettuce turnip the beet on picky eating! | A pun, and an exclamation mark |
| Kids, am I right? 🙄 | At the child's expense, emoji as the joke |
| Don't worry, we've all been terrible parents at dinner. | At the parent's expense |
| Your fussy eater might just be the pickiest kid in the Netherlands. | A label, a joke about the child |
| Q: Does a lick count? A: Only if your toddler is a cat. | Humour in a FAQ |
| Mealtime meltdowns got you down? We feel you! | Chirpy, a rhetorical question, an exclamation mark |
| Nothing logged tonight? Someone's been naughty. | Guilt, diet-culture word, in an empty state |

---

## Per locale

- **NL.** Understatement travels well. Keep it flat and factual; the smile is in the precision.
- **IT.** Warmth rather than dryness. A line that puts the nonna's plate and the pediatra's advice
  in the same breath is fine. Never irony about the family itself.
- **EN.** A dry British register works for this audience on both sides of the Atlantic. Avoid
  sitcom rhythm and "we feel you".
