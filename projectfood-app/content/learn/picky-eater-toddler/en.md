A picky eater toddler is, most of the time, a toddler doing what the age asks. Between 2 and 6, children eat fewer foods, turn down what is new and hold on to what they know. Your child does not hate carrots. Your child has met a carrot four times and needs about ten more introductions. The wider picture is in [how to get kids to eat vegetables](/en/learn/learn-to-eat-everything); this page is about where ordinary ends and how to widen the plate.

## What ordinary picky eating looks like at 2 or 3

Ordinary picky eating is narrow, but it is stable. A child who eats about 20 different foods, grows along their own curve and has energy for the day is inside the normal range, even if the list looks short next to your own plate.

These all fit the ordinary picture:

- Eating fewer foods than a year ago.
- Refusing something new before it reaches the fork.
- Wanting the same meal, the same cup or the same order on the plate.
- Accepting a food on Tuesday and refusing it on Wednesday.
- Liking a food only in one form, such as plain pasta or raw carrot.

The list matters more than any single dinner. Write down everything eaten across one week, snacks and drinks included, and count the different foods. Most parents are surprised by the number, in a good way.

## Why a picky eater toddler stops at a few foods

Three things work at once, and all three belong to the age. Around the second year, new food starts to feel risky. This is called food neophobia, it is strongest between 2 and 6, and for most children it fades on its own (Dovey, 2008). Many vegetables are also slightly bitter, and children taste bitter more strongly than adults do. And a toddler finds out that saying no works, and the plate is one of the few places where that no has an instant effect.

None of this comes from how you cook or how you parent. It shows up in households that do everything by the book. What you can influence is whether eating turns into a fight, and that is where the progress comes from. There is more on the first of the three in the article on [food neophobia](/en/learn/learn-to-eat-everything/food-neophobia).

## How to widen the plate one taste at a time

Pick one food and stay with it for weeks. Variety feels like progress, but it restarts the count every time. Put a small piece on a separate saucer next to the plate, beside something your child already eats. Say one thing about it at most, then leave it alone, and eat the same food yourself.

Count on 8 to 15 tastes before a new food becomes an easy one, sometimes more. In a home trial with children aged 2 to 6, one small taste of a disliked vegetable every day for two weeks was enough for most of them to start liking it (Wardle, 2003). A lick counts, and so does a bite that comes back out, because the flavour has already made contact.

Take small steps within a family of foods. A child who eats only plain pasta might meet the same pasta in a new shape, then a spoon of sauce beside it, then something inside the sauce. A child who lives on bread might meet a new topping on the same slice. Each step stays small enough not to feel new.

## What backfires

- **Forcing or bargaining.** "Three more bites" turns eating into a trade, and the new food into the price.
- **Cooking a separate meal.** A plate of only familiar foods removes every reason to try anything else.
- **Dessert as the reward.** It makes dessert worth more and the vegetable worth less.
- **The clean plate rule.** It puts the amount at the centre, while liking a food starts with the flavour in the first bite.
- **Comparing with a sibling or a friend.** Nobody learns to like a food in order to beat someone.
- **Trying something different every day.** Repetition of one food does the work, and variety resets it.

## A child who eats mostly pasta and bread

Keep serving them. A child who eats 4 foods loses nothing by seeing a fifth on the table, and taking away the foods that do get eaten only raises the stakes. Put the familiar food down as usual and place one small new thing beside it each time.

There is more on the youngest end of the range, the toddler who suddenly eats very little, in the article on [why a toddler won't eat](/en/learn/learn-to-eat-everything/toddler-wont-eat).

## When to ask for help

Talk to your doctor if your child is losing weight or not growing, eats fewer than about 20 different foods, refuses whole groups such as all vegetables or all fruit, gags or panics at new food, or if the worry is getting too big for you. The word ARFID sometimes comes up online: only a doctor or a dietitian can say whether anything like it applies, and this page does not try to. It is a different situation from ordinary fussiness, and there is help for it. Asking a year too early is better than a year too late.

## When does picky eating stop

For most children it eases after 6. Refusing new foods peaks between 2 and 6 and then declines (Dovey, 2008). What sets the pace is mostly how the table feels. A child who hears every evening that they eat too little remembers the tension, not the food. A child who meets a small taste at a calm table tends to move a little faster.

Some children stay careful eaters for good. That is a style, and a narrow style is workable as long as growth, energy and a few foods from each group are in place.
